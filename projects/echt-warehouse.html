Project Overview

Summary

Identified an inefficiency on the warehouse floor and worked with a third-party WMS vendor to consolidate three fragmented inventory tables into a single intuitive view, reducing cognitive load and bridging the skill gap between experienced and new staff.

My Role

I acted as the operational subject-matter expert and liaison between ECHT management and the third-party WMS provider. I defined the required business logic, documented the solution, validated the live implementation and trained staff on the updated workflow.

My Contributions

  • Identified the inefficiency and pitched the consolidation to management and the WMS vendor (Thomax Technology)
  • Authored the full technical specification: calculation logic for replenResidual, multi-condition filtering rules, edge case handling (negative values become 0, positives are bolded), and an annotated example table, submitted directly to the vendor developer
  • Caught a live-data bug mid-day after go-live: replenResidual values were static snapshots not recalculating on BulkBinCode updates, then documented the root cause and escalated it for a dynamic fix
  • Trained staff on the new workflow with practical tips and best practices

The Problem

Fragmented Warehouse Workflow

The warehouse relied on three separate tables that staff had to cross-reference simultaneously:

  1. Inventory pallet contents (what items are where)
  2. Collection status (items picked and ready)
  3. Customer order tracking (item locations for the day)

This forced staff to mentally consolidate data across three views, increasing cognitive load and error risk during high-volume operations.

Solution

Consolidated Operational View

I proposed replacing the three-report workflow with one consolidated operational view, and worked with ECHT management and Thomax Technology to translate the process into clear technical requirements ahead of the vendor's 11 December 2023 deployment.

  • Mapped variables and dependencies to understand the full data landscape
  • Identified essential vs. redundant fields to reduce cognitive load
  • Coordinated specification and vendor delivery across tight deadlines

Technical Spec

Replenishment Logic

The spec I authored and submitted to the vendor defined the logic for a new replenResidual column:

replenResidual = ReplenQuantity
− SUM(InventoryQuantity where BulkBinCode starts with “SUP” or equals “TABLE”)

Display Rules

  1. Calculate the residual.
  2. Clamp negative values to zero.
  3. Hide records where the final residual equals zero.
  4. Bold positive values.

Annotated Example (Search input: TRA-FA-19%)

The table is horizontally scrollable on smaller screens. Codes use monospace formatting and the visible/hidden column shows the final display state after the residual rules are applied.

Deployment Defect and Diagnosis

Live Validation & Defect Diagnosis

During validation, I found records showing replenishment quantities that should already have resolved to zero.

I traced the behaviour to values being calculated as static snapshots rather than recalculating after inventory-location updates.

I documented the observed behaviour and likely root cause, then escalated it to the vendor for correction before the Christmas peak.

Results & Impact

Primary impact: Enabled one operator to coordinate work that had previously required two separate workflows at nearly the same speed.

What Changed

  • Consolidated system, three fragmented WMS views into one operational report
  • Fewer errors, reduced manual cross-referencing, duplicated movement and the risk of missed replenishment items
  • Faster onboarding, simplified onboarding by reducing the number of reports and rules new staff needed to learn
  • Validation, identified a stale-data defect during live validation and escalated it before the Christmas peak

Estimated operational outcomes included a 60–80% improvement in retrieval and stock-movement efficiency, approximately 50% faster training and roughly 40% fewer workflow errors.
