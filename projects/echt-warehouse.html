Overview

How the Project Started

The project started after I heard repeated frustration about having to check three different WMS tables to confirm inventory information. Experienced staff knew which values to compare and where to look, but the process was difficult for newer staff to learn and easy to get wrong.

I translated that operational problem into a technical specification for the WMS software developer. I mapped how the three tables related, identified the fields and calculations needed from each, defined filtering and edge-case behaviour, and described how they should be combined into one reliable operational view.

From Operational Problem to Technical Design

  • Understand the data: traced how fields across three WMS tables related and which values depended on one another.
  • Define the logic: specified the calculations, filters and edge cases required for a reliable combined view.
  • Translate requirements: turned warehouse staff needs into implementation requirements the WMS developer could build from.

The Problem

Translating Operational Needs

Staff could describe the warehouse problem, but the software needed precise rules for what data mattered, how the fields related, what should be calculated, and what should appear in the final view.

Understanding the Data Relationships

I had to identify which fields across the three WMS tables affected replenishment accuracy and efficiency, how those values depended on one another, and which calculations, filters and edge cases were required for a reliable combined view.

Delivering on Time

The solution needed to be defined, implemented and checked within roughly one week before the Christmas peak. That made it important to keep the requirements clear, validate the calculations carefully, and make sure the new view was reliable before staff depended on it.

Technical Design

Selecting the Relevant Data

I reviewed the available WMS reports to determine which data was actually needed for replenishment. Some information overlapped across reports, so I narrowed the workflow down to three main tables and identified the fields required for the combined view.

Defining the Combined View

I mapped the relationships between those fields, defined new calculated values where the existing reports did not provide what staff needed, and specified which information should appear in the final operational view.

Setting Clear Rules Before Implementation

Before implementation, I documented the calculations, filtering behaviour, edge cases and expected outputs so the WMS software developer had clear rules to build and validate against.

For example, stock already moved into replenishment locations should reduce the remaining quantity shown to staff, and rows with nothing left to replenish should no longer appear in the view.

Deployment Defect and Diagnosis

Live Validation & Defect Diagnosis

During validation, I found records showing replenishment quantities that should already have resolved to zero.

I traced the behaviour to values being calculated as static snapshots rather than recalculating after inventory-location updates.

I documented the observed behaviour and likely root cause, then escalated it to the WMS software developer for correction before the Christmas peak.

Operational Impact

The combined view was used to make replenishment decisions, so stale or incorrect values could have immediate operational consequences. If stock was not replenished correctly, pickers and packers could arrive at an empty location, delaying orders and creating backlog for the following day.

Timing made this more important before Christmas. A mistake late in the week could carry into Monday because the warehouse did not operate over the weekend, increasing pressure on the busiest day and sometimes requiring additional casual labour to recover the backlog.

Reducing repeated cross-checking also freed experienced staff to spend more time on picking, packing, receiving inventory, and other warehouse work during the peak period.

Results & Impact

Primary impact: Enabled one operator to coordinate work that had previously required two separate workflows at nearly the same speed.

What Changed

  • Consolidated system, three fragmented WMS views into one operational report
  • Fewer errors, reduced manual cross-referencing, duplicated movement and the risk of missed replenishment items
  • Faster onboarding, simplified onboarding by reducing the number of reports and rules new staff needed to learn
  • Validation, identified a stale-data defect during live validation and escalated it before the Christmas peak

Estimated Outcomes

  • 60–80% faster retrieval, improving how quickly staff could confirm inventory and stock movements.
  • ~50% faster onboarding, because new staff had fewer reports and rules to learn.
  • ~40% fewer errors, from reducing repeated cross-checking and inconsistent interpretation across views.
